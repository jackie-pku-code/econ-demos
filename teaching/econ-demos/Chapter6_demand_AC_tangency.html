Why, mathematically, does tangency between the demand curve and the average cost curve imply MR = MC?

Since C(Q) = AC(Q) · Q and R(Q) = P(Q) · Q, differentiating with respect to Q gives:

MC=∂C∂Q= ∂(AC·Q)∂Q= ∂AC∂Q·Q+AC
MR=∂R∂Q= ∂(P(Q)·Q)∂Q= ∂P(Q)∂Q·Q+P

Given this, if any two of the facts below are true, then the remaining one will be true as well (at the same Q > 0):

  1. MR = MC (Profit maximization)
  2. AC = P (Zero profit)
  3. ∂AC/∂Q = ∂P/∂Q (Average cost curve tangent with the demand curve)
